> [!|255, 208, 0] [[IPCC_DA_Good_Practice_Guidance_Hegerl_et_al_2010.pdf#page=3&annotation=133R|p.3]]
> > Detection of change is defined as the process of demonstrating that [[climate]] or a system affected by climate is changed in some defined statistical sense without providing reason or cause for that change 

> [!|255, 208, 0] [[IPCC_DA_Good_Practice_Guidance_Hegerl_et_al_2010.pdf#page=3&annotation=136R|p.3]]
> > An identified change has occurred in observation if the likelihood of occurrence by change due to [[Internal Variability|internal variability]] alone is determined to be small

> [!PDF|red] [[IPCC_DA_Good_Practice_Guidance_Hegerl_et_al_2010.pdf#page=3&selection=239,0,266,5&color=yellow|p.3]]
> > Attribution is defined as the process of evaluating the relative contributions of multiple causal factors to a change or event with an assignment of statistical confidence

> [!PDF|red] [[IPCC_DA_Good_Practice_Guidance_Hegerl_et_al_2010.pdf#page=3&selection=320,0,330,1&color=yellow|p.3]]
> > Attribution seeks to determine whether a specified set of external forcings and/or drivers are the cause of an observed change in a specific system. 
> 

## List of D&A approaches
### Single-step Attribution to External Forcings

> [!PDF|blue] [[IPCC_DA_Good_Practice_Guidance_Hegerl_et_al_2010.pdf#page=3&selection=446,0,455,50&color=yellow|p.3]]
> > This method comprises assessments that attribute an observed change within a system to an external forcing based on explicitly modelling the response of the variable to external forcings and drivers

> [!PDF|blue] [[IPCC_DA_Good_Practice_Guidance_Hegerl_et_al_2010.pdf#page=4&selection=27,0,40,22&color=yellow|p.4]]
> > The attribution step involves detection of a significant change in the variable of interest and comparison of observed changes in the variable of interest with expected changes due to external forcings and drivers (typically derived from modelling approaches).

### Multi-Step Attribution to External Forcings

> [!PDF|blue] [[IPCC_DA_Good_Practice_Guidance_Hegerl_et_al_2010.pdf#page=4&selection=58,0,88,5&color=yellow|p.4]]
> > This method comprises assessments that attribute an observed change in a variable of interest to a change in climate and/or environmental conditions, plus separate assessments that attribute the change in climate and/or environmental conditions to external drivers and external forcings. 

> [!PDF|blue] [[IPCC_DA_Good_Practice_Guidance_Hegerl_et_al_2010.pdf#page=4&selection=115,19,127,45&color=yellow|p.4]]
> > In the case of climate extremes and rare events, for example, it may not always be possible to reliably estimate from observations whether there has been a change in frequency or intensity of a given type of event.

### Associative Pattern Attribution to External Forcings

> [!PDF|blue] [[IPCC_DA_Good_Practice_Guidance_Hegerl_et_al_2010.pdf#page=5&selection=28,0,50,11&color=yellow|p.5]]
> > This method comprises a synthesis of large numbers of results (possibly across multiple systems) demonstrating the sensitivity of impacts to a change in climate conditions and other external drivers
 
### Attribution to a Change in Climatic Conditions

> [!PDF|blue] [[IPCC_DA_Good_Practice_Guidance_Hegerl_et_al_2010.pdf#page=5&selection=95,0,103,44&color=yellow|p.5]]
> > This method comprises assessments that attribute an observed change in a variable of interest to an observed change in climate conditions. 

> [!PDF|blue] [[IPCC_DA_Good_Practice_Guidance_Hegerl_et_al_2010.pdf#page=5&selection=115,0,130,7&color=yellow|p.5]]
> > This method can be the final step in Multi-Step Attribution, but it can also be used stand-alone to address climate impacts on a variable of interest.


> [!PDF|blue] [[IPCC_DA_Good_Practice_Guidance_Hegerl_et_al_2010.pdf#page=5&selection=150,22,201,22&color=yellow|p.5]]
> > Confidence in assessments will be increased when attribution of change to a causal factor is robustly quantified and when there is firm understanding of the processes (‘process knowledge’) that are involved in a proposed causal link (e.g., the link between elevated temperature and declining crop yields is strengthened by understanding of the stress physiology of plants).
